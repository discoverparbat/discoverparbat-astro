Overview

About Kokhe Danda Trek

Kokhe Danda (3,309 m) is the highest point of Parbat district, and the Kokhe Danda Trek is a 5-day, easy trek from Pokhara that reaches it through Magar villages, rhododendron and oak forest and family-run homestays. The ridge sits next to Mohare Danda, which lies across the boundary in Myagdi district, and close to Poon Hill, but it sees far fewer trekkers, with views of Dhaulagiri, Annapurna and, on clear days, Manaslu and Langtang.

The Kokhe Danda Trek is a relatively new trail, opened by us at Discover Parbat. We built it to show trekkers the Parbat we know: quiet forest trails, hilltop villages and families who still live much as their grandparents did. What sets it apart from better-known routes is not only the scenery but the chance to step into local culture and daily life rather than simply passing through it.

Your first night is at Haljure, in a truly authentic homestay. This is a traditional village home, not a lodge built for tourists. Dinner is cooked by your host in a traditional Nepali kitchen, often over a wood fire, and you are welcome to sit beside the hearth while dal bhat, seasonal vegetables and fresh rotis are prepared. After the meal, a campfire is lit outside and, with almost no light pollution, the night sky fills with stars.

At Lespar, you can join a momo-making session with your hosts, learning to fold Nepal's favourite dumplings by hand before eating them together. Spend free time exploring the village on foot, walking between terraced fields and stone-and-mud houses, and chatting with locals about farming, festivals and life in the hills. These small, unplanned moments are often what trekkers remember most.

Because the trail is still new, it sees very few tourists. On most departures you will have the trail and the homestays practically to yourselves, with no queues at viewpoints, no crowded dining halls and no competition for the best seat at sunrise. It is a rare kind of solitude in the Annapurna foothills, only a short drive from Pokhara.

Our guides are local to the region and were part of the team that built this trail. They know every path, village and family along the route, and they bring deep knowledge of the area's history, plants, wildlife and Magar traditions. Walking with them feels less like a guided tour and more like being shown around by friends from home.

The reward at the top is a sweeping Himalayan panorama. From Kokhe Danda, the view stretches from Dhaulagiri and the Annapurna range across to Manaslu and, on clear days, as far as the Langtang range. Sunrise paints the snow peaks gold while the valleys below are still in shadow, and sunsets from the ridge are just as unforgettable. It is one of the few places in the Annapurna foothills where you can take in Nepal's mountains far from the crowds. You may also see the ridge written as Kokhe Dada or Khoke Danda; all of these names refer to the same place.

Plan Your Trek

Best Time, Cost and Getting There

Best time: October and November give the clearest views, and March to May brings rhododendron blooms. See our month-by-month guide to the best time for Kokhe Danda.

Cost: the trek costs $350 to $410 per person depending on group size. It includes private transport, meals, accommodation, guide and porter. See the cost table.

Getting there: we drive from Pokhara to Banau via Kushma in about 2.5 hours, then walk to Haljure. The trek ends at Mallaj, with a taxi back to Pokhara via Kushma.

Day by Day

Detailed Itinerary

Morning drive from Pokhara to Kushma, a one and a half hour drive (57KM). Exploration of Kushma, city of adventure. If you would be interested, you can also do Bungee or Zipline. We have lunch at Kushma and then drive one hour further to Banau, 2100m. From Banau, we start our hike and after 1–1.5hrs of hike, we reach our Homestay at Haljure.

Altitude: 2,445mDistance: 3 KMTime: 1.5 HrsStay: Homestay

We wake up early in the morning for sunrise view. During clear weather, we can see from Dhaulagiri, Annapurna, Mansiri, Ganesh to Langtang range peaks.

We have local breakfast and after that we start our hike to Lespar. At Lespar, we will have a Momo making session with the host at Homestay. We walk for around an hour to explore the village, interact with locals and you can also try the Magar Cultural Dress as well. After lunch, we hike to Sarga Camp and Homestay.

Altitude: 2,400mDistance: 8 KMTime: 4–5 HrsStay: Homestay / Camping

Even though we do not see snowcapped peaks from Sarga, this is still a charming place inside the forest. After breakfast, we set off to Kokhe Danda. The first half till lunch is a bit steep where we gain around 700m in elevation, which takes around 3–3.5 hrs. We pass through beautiful Rhododendron and Oak forests. After lunch at Dhima, it's more comfortable as we hike through a gradual uphill section except for the 10 and 5 minute uphill before reaching Mohare and Kokhe Danda.

Altitude: 3,309mDistance: 8.2 KMTime: 6–7 HrsStay: Tea House

We wake up early in the morning for sunrise and mountain views. On clear weather, we can see close views of Dhaulagiri and Annapurna Range mountains.

After breakfast, we start our hike towards Phalame Danda. Initially, we hike down to Nangi village, where we stop for lunch. After that, we hike gradually up for the next half and then mostly flat before we reach our Community Homestay at Phalame Danda.

Altitude: 2,300mDistance: 14 KMTime: 7 HrsStay: Community Homestay

On the last day, we still see both Dhaulagiri and a few peaks of the Annapurna range. Initially, we hike down to Banskharka village where we stop for a tea break. After that we hike down to Mallaj where we end our trek and take a taxi back to Pokhara via Kushma.

Altitude: 1,000mDistance: 7 KMTime: 4 Hrs
Common Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

No prior trekking experience is needed. Kokhe Danda is rated Easy and is suitable for first-time trekkers. The longest day (Day 3) involves around 6–7 hours of walking with some elevation gain, so a basic level of fitness helps — but you don't need to be an athlete. If you can walk comfortably for a few hours, you'll be fine.

We arrange private transport from Pokhara to Banau (approx. 2.5 hours including the stop in Kushma). The drive is scenic — you pass through the Kali Gandaki gorge area and arrive in the hills of Parbat. No public buses or connections to worry about; it's all handled by us.

The homestays are simple, clean, and genuinely family-run — this is one of the things that makes Kokhe Danda special. You'll typically have a private or semi-private room with basic bedding. Bathrooms are usually shared and facilities are basic. Think warm hospitality over luxury. Meals are home-cooked by the host family, which is honestly one of the highlights of the trip.

You'll eat what the local families eat — mostly dal bhat (lentils and rice), fresh vegetables, local honey, eggs, and seasonal produce. On Day 2, there's a hands-on momo making session with your host family. It's wholesome, filling, and genuinely delicious. If you have dietary restrictions or allergies, let us know in advance and we'll make sure the hosts are prepared.

Mobile signal (NCell or NTC) is available in most villages along the route, though it can be patchy on the higher ridgelines. Wi-Fi is not reliably available at the homestays — this is a remote trail and part of its charm. We'd recommend downloading offline maps and letting family know you may be out of contact for stretches. A local SIM card from Pokhara is a good idea if you need connectivity.

The two peak windows are March–May (spring) and October–November (autumn). Spring brings rhododendrons in full bloom along the forest trails, while autumn delivers the clearest mountain views after the monsoon clears the air. December is quieter than most people expect — fewer trekkers and crisp visibility make it a genuinely underrated option. June–September is the monsoon season; trails are lush with waterfalls and wildflowers but wetter underfoot.

Absolutely — solo trekkers are very welcome and actually quite common on this route. You'll have a private guide for the full trek, and the solo experience on these quiet trails is genuinely special. For a 5-day easy trek, you don't need to overpack. Essentials include: sturdy walking shoes or light trekking boots, layers for cool evenings at altitude (3,300m nights can be cold), a rain jacket, a good daypack, a reusable water bottle, sunscreen, and a headlamp. A sleeping bag liner is useful for homestay nights. We'll send you a full packing list after booking.

If you need to return early for any reason, there is no refund on the remaining trek cost. However, we will arrange comfortable accommodation for you in Pokhara for the number of nights that remain on your itinerary — at no extra charge. Please also see our full Cancellation Policy for details.
