Overview

About Panchase Trek

The Panchase Trek is a three-day walk to one of the finest viewpoints near Pokhara, built around a night with a Gurung family and a sunrise few trekkers ever see. Panchase sits where Kaski, Parbat and Syangja districts meet, and from its top the Himalaya spreads across the whole northern sky: Dhaulagiri, the Annapurna range, Machhapuchhre and, on clear mornings, Manaslu far to the east, with the Pokhara valley and rolling layers of hills falling away to the south.

We start on the Parbat side, the way we know best. After a scenic drive from Pokhara with a stop in Kushma, the trek begins at Dandakharka, a Gurung village of slate-roofed houses and terraced fields with a small gumba at its heart. From there it is a slow, easy hour on foot to Arthar Badagaun, an old Gurung settlement where half-abandoned stone houses stand beside the homes that are still lived in. The flagstone lanes, carved windows and whitewashed walls with red-earth trim are some of the best-kept traditional Gurung architecture in the region, and you spend the night here in a family homestay, eating what the family eats.

The second day is the heart of the trek: four to five hours through oak and rhododendron forest, where the old oaks are hung with orchids and moss, past the Panchakoshi pond and quiet village viewpoints, up to Panchase itself. The trail is peaceful and shaded, and you are more likely to meet a herder or a pilgrim than another trekking group. Accommodation on the top is a simple local lodge, basic but warm, and perfectly placed for the morning.

Sunrise from Panchase is the reason people come back. The snow peaks light up one by one while the valleys below are often still full of cloud, and on the southern side the hills stack up in layer after layer of blue. After breakfast we walk down through Bhanjyang to Ghatichhina, and an hour's drive brings you back to Pokhara by the afternoon.

With short walking days, a maximum altitude of around 2,500 m and no long climbs, the Panchase Trek suits first-time trekkers, families and anyone with only a few days, and it is one of the best short treks from Pokhara outside the busy Poon Hill trail. Want more time in Parbat? Our Kokhe Danda Trek takes five days to the highest point of the district, with Magar homestays in Haljure and Lespar.

Plan Your Trek

Best Time, Cost and Getting There

Best time: October to December brings the clearest mountain views and dry trails, and is the best season for the Panchase sunrise. March to May is the other great window, when the rhododendron forest on day two is in bloom, although afternoons can be hazier. Winter nights on the top are cold, so bring a warm layer. In the June to September monsoon the forest is lush but views are often hidden by cloud and the trails can be slippery and leechy.

Cost: the trek runs for a minimum of two people: US$260 per person for 2, US$249 per person for 3 to 4, and US$230 per person for groups of 5 or more. The price includes private transport both ways, your guide, meals and accommodation on the trek. Private departures can start on any date.

Getting there: we pick you up in Pokhara and drive about three hours to Dandakharka, with a stop in Kushma, the headquarters of Parbat district. The trek ends at Ghatichhina, about an hour's drive from Pokhara, so there is no need to walk back the way you came.

Day by Day

Detailed Itinerary

We leave Pokhara in the morning and drive about three hours into Parbat district, with a tea stop in Kushma, the district headquarters perched above the Kali Gandaki and Modi rivers. The road climbs through terraced hills to Dandakharka, a Gurung village of slate-roofed houses and golden terraces.

Before we start walking, we take time to explore Dandakharka and its small gumba (monastery). Then it is a slow, easy hour on foot to Arthar Badagaun, an old Gurung village where half-abandoned stone houses stand beside family homes that are still lived in. Wander the flagstone lanes, look at the carved windows and red-earth trims, and spend the evening with your host family in an authentic local homestay.

Time: 3 Hrs drive · 1 Hr hikeStay: Homestay

Today is the most beautiful walk of the trek. The trail climbs gently through oak and rhododendron forest, where many of the old oak trees are covered in orchids and moss, with village views opening up between the trees. It is a quiet, shaded and peaceful path.

We pass the Panchakoshi pond on the way and continue up to Panchase, arriving in time to watch the afternoon light on the mountains. Tonight we stay in a simple local lodge on the top: basic, but warm and perfectly placed for sunrise.

Time: 4–5 HrsStay: Basic lodge

We wake early for sunrise from Panchase. As the sky brightens, Dhaulagiri, the Annapurna range, Machhapuchhre and, on clear days, Manaslu light up across the north, with the Pokhara valley below and layer after layer of hills stretching away to the south.

After breakfast we hike down through Bhanjyang to Ghatichhina, mostly downhill through forest and farmland. From Ghatichhina it is about an hour's drive back to Pokhara, arriving in the afternoon.

Time: 3–4 Hrs hike · 1 Hr drive
