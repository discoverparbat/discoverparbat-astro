Multiple Viewpoints Trek: The Best Alternative to the Annapurna Circuit

Big Annapurna and Dhaulagiri views, quiet trails and village hospitality over 14 days, with no high pass to cross.

The short answer

The Annapurna Circuit is one of Nepal's most famous treks, but it is not the right fit for everyone. Roads now cut through parts of the route, the main trail is busy in peak season, and the Thorong La crossing at 5,416 m means altitude is a serious concern.

If you want a long, rewarding Annapurna-region journey without those drawbacks, the Multiple Viewpoints Trek is one of the best alternatives to the Annapurna Circuit Trek. Designed by Discover Parbat, this 14-day trek joins Mardi Himal, Ghandruk, Muldai Viewpoint, Khopra Ridge, Khayer Lake, Poon Hill and Kokhe Danda into one continuous route.

At a glance

Duration: 14 days, Pokhara to Pokhara. Difficulty: moderate. High pass: none. Viewpoints: Mardi Himal ridge, Muldai, Khopra Ridge, Poon Hill and Kokhe Danda. Stays: homestays and teahouses.

Every day brings a different angle on Annapurna, Dhaulagiri, Machhapuchhre and Nilgiri.

Why look beyond the Annapurna Circuit?

The Circuit deserves its reputation for dramatic changes in scenery, from subtropical valleys to the high, dry landscape near Manang and Muktinath. But many trekkers now look for something different, for three main reasons.

  • Altitude. Thorong La at 5,416 m is a long, demanding day and the main reason people turn back.
  • Roads. Road construction has changed parts of the route, so some sections mix trail with vehicle tracks.
  • Crowds. The main trail is busy in the spring and autumn peaks.

You do not have to give up big mountain views to avoid these. The hills of Parbat and the southern Annapurna ridges offer views of the same giants, with a very different feel on the trail.

What is the Multiple Viewpoints Trek?

The Multiple Viewpoints Trek is a 14-day circuit through the Annapurna region that starts and ends in Pokhara. It combines famous highlights such as Poon Hill and Mardi Himal with quieter places such as Khopra Ridge, Muldai Viewpoint and Kokhe Danda, which most trekkers never see.

The route climbs through rhododendron and oak forest to the Mardi Himal ridge, drops to the Gurung village of Ghandruk, then crosses to Dobato, Khopra Ridge and Khayer Lake. It then descends to Swanta, joins the classic trail at Ghorepani and Poon Hill, and finishes with the little-visited ridge to Kokhe Danda, Lespar and Banau.

8 reasons it is a great Annapurna Circuit alternative

1. Many sunrise viewpoints in one trek

On the Circuit, the big moment is the pass. Here you get a series of them: sunrise from Muldai Viewpoint, Poon Hill and Kokhe Danda, plus ridge walking on Mardi Himal and Khopra Ridge. Each one shows Annapurna and Dhaulagiri from a different direction, which is exactly why the trek is called "multiple viewpoints".

2. Lower altitude, no high-pass crossing

There is no Thorong La on this route. The highest point is the optional day hike to Khayer Lake, a high alpine lake at roughly 4,600 m, which you reach after sleeping at Khopra Ridge and return from the same day. That gives your body time to adjust and makes the trek far more approachable for first-time Himalayan trekkers.

3. Far fewer crowds

Poon Hill and the Annapurna Circuit are popular. Khopra Ridge, Muldai and Kokhe Danda are not. On this route you can enjoy long stretches of quiet trail and have a viewpoint largely to yourself, especially beyond Poon Hill, where the route heads into rarely visited hills.

4. Foot trails, not road walking

This trek follows ridges, forest paths, terraced fields and village trails. A vehicle is used only at the start (Pokhara to Kande) and at the end (Banau to Pokhara via Kushma).

5. Real Gurung and Magar village culture

You pass through Ghandruk, Swanta, Chitre, Lespar and other hill villages where traditional Gurung and Magar life continues. You stay in homestays and teahouses, eat local food and can join activities such as momo-making sessions, so your trip supports local families directly.

6. Variety from forest to alpine ridge

In two weeks you walk through oak and rhododendron forest, terraced farmland, open alpine ridges and a sacred high lake. If you love the changing scenery of the Circuit, this route offers similar diversity without crossing a pass.

7. A sacred highlight: Khayer Lake

The day hike from Khopra Ridge to Khayer Lake is a highlight. The lake sits in dramatic mountain scenery and holds spiritual importance for local people, which gives the trek meaning as well as beauty.

8. Led by a local Parbat team

Discover Parbat is based in the Parbat region. Our team knows the trails, the lodge owners and the best light at each viewpoint, including hidden sections such as Kokhe Danda and Lespar that large operators often skip.

Who is this trek right for?

  • Trekkers who want Poon Hill and Khopra Ridge without the usual crowds
  • Anyone with about two weeks who wants forest, ridge and village scenery in one trip
  • People who prefer a lower-altitude alternative to the Annapurna Circuit
  • Photographers who want several different sunrise locations
  • Travelers who enjoy authentic culture, homestays, local food and momo-making

It may not suit you if you only have 7 to 8 days, or if you expect luxury lodges throughout. Accommodation is comfortable but simple. For a shorter trip, ask us about a shorter version of the route.

Best time for the Multiple Viewpoints Trek

Spring (March to May) and autumn (late September to November) are the best seasons. Spring brings blooming rhododendrons in the forests, and autumn offers the clearest skies after the monsoon. Nights on the higher ridges can be cold in both seasons, so read our Khopra Ridge packing guide before you go.

Frequently asked questions

Is the Multiple Viewpoints Trek a good alternative to the Annapurna Circuit Trek?

Yes. It gives you Annapurna, Dhaulagiri, Machhapuchhre and Nilgiri views, plus village culture and varied landscapes over 14 days, with no high pass, fewer crowds and mostly foot trails.

How difficult is the Multiple Viewpoints Trek?

It is graded moderate. Expect several hours of walking each day, with some steep ascents and descents. Good general fitness is enough, and no technical climbing is involved.

How long is the Multiple Viewpoints Trek?

The full trek is 14 days from Pokhara back to Pokhara. A shorter version is available if you cannot spare two weeks.

Which viewpoints are included?

The route includes the Mardi Himal ridge, Muldai Viewpoint, Khopra Ridge, the Khayer Lake day hike, Poon Hill and Kokhe Danda.

What is the accommodation like?

You stay in homestays and teahouses. They are clean and comfortable but simple, so do not expect luxury lodges throughout.

Is the Multiple Viewpoints Trek less crowded than the Annapurna Circuit?

Yes. Poon Hill sees regular visitors, but Khopra Ridge, Muldai and the section to Kokhe Danda and Lespar are much quieter.

Final thoughts

If you want big mountains, quiet trails and genuine village hospitality, the Multiple Viewpoints Trek delivers much of what the Annapurna region is loved for, without the high pass, the road sections or the crowds. It is a longer, more varied journey that combines famous highlights with hidden routes.
