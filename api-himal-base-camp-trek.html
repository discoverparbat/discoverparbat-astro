Overview

About Api Himal Base Camp Trek

The Api Himal Base Camp Trek takes you to the foot of Api (7,132 m), the highest mountain in far-western Nepal, following the Chameliya River through Darchula district and into the Api Nampa Conservation Area. Our 14-day journey begins with a morning flight from Kathmandu to Nepalgunj and two days in Bardiya National Park, climbs through the villages of Ghusa and Siti to the high pastures of Dhauli Odhar and Api Base Camp (3,820 m), and returns by road through Lumbini, the birthplace of the Buddha, to Pokhara.

This is one of the least visited corners of the Nepal Himalaya. Far-western Nepal has seen very little trekking development, and there is no chain of lodges with printed menus waiting at the end of each day. You sleep in a family homestay at Siti, in a simple stone-hut lodge at Shimar, and in a basic teahouse or tents at base camp. For the right traveller, that is exactly the appeal: on most days the only people you meet on the trail are villagers, herders and porters carrying supplies up the valley.

The first two trekking days pass through farming villages where life still follows the seasons. Stone-and-timber houses sit above terraced fields, and the evening at Siti is spent with a host family, sharing dal bhat cooked over the fire and learning a little about life this far from the nearest road. From Siti the trail follows the Chameliya upstream, and for much of the next day the sound of the river rising from the gorge below stays with you.

Above Shimar the forest opens into wide pastures, and at Dhauli Odhar the valley widens into a broad, grassy shelf below the mountains. A short, gentle climb leads on to Api Base Camp, where the great wall of Api fills the head of the valley and the neighbouring peaks of Nampa and Bobaye catch the last light of the evening. With no towns for days in any direction, the night sky here is extraordinary.

We spend a full day exploring the base camp area: walking up to where the Chameliya, known locally as the Chaulani, begins below the glaciers, visiting the small lake of Ringdepani and climbing to Kalidhunga Lake at 4,160 m, the highest point of the trek. It is a landscape of moraine ridges, boulder fields and cold, clear water, framed on every side by snow peaks.

Reaching Darchula means long days on the road, so we have built the journey around them. On the way in, two days in Bardiya National Park give you a chance to look for tigers, rhinos and wild elephants in one of Nepal's quietest national parks. On the way back, a day in Lumbini lets you walk among the monasteries and the sacred garden where the Buddha was born before the final drive to Pokhara.

This is a remote trek and it asks for more patience and preparation than the busy routes around Annapurna or Everest. Facilities are simple, the days are long and plans sometimes have to bend to weather or road conditions. In return you get a part of Nepal that very few trekkers ever see, walked with a team that knows the trail and the families along it.

Plan Your Trek

Best Time, Cost and Getting There

Best time: October and November bring the clearest skies and the best views of Api. March to May is warmer, with flowering forests on the lower trail. Winter brings snow and freezing nights at base camp, and the monsoon from June to September makes trails slippery and roads unreliable.

Cost: the trek runs with a minimum of two people and costs $1,399 per person for two, $1,240 per person for three to four and $1,200 per person for five or more. See the cost table for what is included.

Getting there: we take a morning flight from Kathmandu to Nepalgunj and drive to Bardiya the same day. If you prefer, you can travel from Kathmandu by road instead. This saves $110 per person but adds one extra day, as the drive is long. The trip ends in Pokhara after a final drive from Lumbini.

Looking for something shorter? Our Kokhe Danda Trek is a 5-day homestay trek from Pokhara, and the Multiple Viewpoints Trek links six Himalayan viewpoints around the Annapurna foothills. For other dates or routes, plan a custom trek with us.

Day by Day

Detailed Itinerary

We take a morning flight from Kathmandu to Nepalgunj, about an hour over the hills, with mountain views to the north on a clear day. From the warm, flat Terai we drive west for around three hours to Thakurdwara, the village at the edge of Bardiya National Park. After settling into our jungle lodge, we take an easy afternoon walk through the nearby Tharu village and down to the riverbank for sunset.

Stay: Jungle LodgeFlight: 1 HrDrive: 3 Hrs

An early start takes us into the park on a full-day jeep safari with a local naturalist. Bardiya is one of the best places in Nepal to look for the Bengal tiger, and its grasslands, sal forest and riverbanks are also home to one-horned rhinos, wild elephants, several kinds of deer and over 400 species of birds. We stop with a packed lunch by the Karnali River, where gharial and mugger crocodiles often bask on the sandbanks.

Stay: Jungle LodgeActivity: Full-day Safari

This morning we explore the jungle on foot with our naturalist guides, learning to read tracks and listen for alarm calls, a very different and more intimate way to experience the park. In the afternoon we visit a Tharu village to see their traditional mud-walled houses and learn about the culture of the people who have lived in these forests for generations. We rest early ahead of a long drive tomorrow.

Stay: Jungle LodgeActivity: Jungle Walk

This is the longest day on the road, around 12 hours. We head west across the Terai, then turn north into the hills through Dadeldhura and Baitadi, winding up and down ridges dotted with villages and temples before dropping into Darchula district. We pass Gokuleshwor and continue up the Chameliya valley to Makarigad, the roadhead where our trek begins.

Altitude: 1,470mStay: Local LodgeDrive: 12 Hrs

The trek begins with a gradual uphill walk past a small settlement. After a couple of hours we cross a bridge over the Chameliya River, and from there it is a steady climb of 1.5 to 2 hours to Ghusa, where we stop for lunch. The afternoon is mostly gradual uphill with a mix of short ups and downs. After passing through Arukhod village we reach Siti, where we spend the night in a family homestay.

Altitude: 2,280mDistance: 14 kmTime: 7 HrsStay: Homestay

We start with a downhill walk to Khaikot. From there the trail climbs, gradually at first and then steeply, to Chhirchhire, where we stop for lunch. Another 1.5 to 2 hours of walking brings us to Shimar. For much of the day we walk with the Chameliya below us, its sound and the cool breeze rising from the river keeping us company. Tonight's lodge is a simple stone hut rather than a proper teahouse.

Altitude: 2,870mDistance: 12 kmTime: 6 HrsStay: Basic Lodge

The trail leaves Shimar gently, then just before Bhalle Khola it turns into a steep climb of about two hours. Above it, the effort pays off: we walk for around an hour across open pastures, mostly flat, to Dhauli Odhar (3,470 m), where we stop for lunch. From Dhauli Odhar it is a gradual 1.5-hour climb to Api Base Camp, where the face of Api rises at the head of the valley.

Altitude: 3,820mDistance: 8 kmTime: 5 HrsStay: Teahouse / Tent

Today we explore the upper valley. We first walk up to the source of the Chameliya River, known locally as the Chaulani, where the stream begins below the glaciers and moraine of Api. From there we continue to Ringdepani Lake, a small, still lake tucked among the ridges.

Returning past base camp, we climb to Kalidhunga Lake at 4,160 m, the highest point of the trek. Around us the landscape is stark and beautiful: grey moraine ridges, boulder fields and patches of alpine grass, with Api, Nampa and Bobaye standing above the valley. After time to take it all in, we descend to Dhauli Odhar for the night, sleeping lower to help our bodies recover.

Time: 6–7 HrsStay: Teahouse / TentMax Altitude: 4,160mSleep: 3,470m

We retrace our steps down the valley, across the pastures, down the steep section to Bhalle Khola and on past Shimar and Chhirchhire. It is a long day, but mostly downhill, and the views look different walking in this direction. We arrive back in Siti for a second night with our homestay family.

Altitude: 2,280mTime: 7–8 HrsStay: Homestay

Our last day on foot takes us back through Arukhod and Ghusa, across the Chameliya bridge and down to Makarigad, where the trek ends. After lunch we drive down the valley to Gokuleshwor for the night, a good place to celebrate with the team.

Distance: 14 kmStay: GuesthouseTrek: 5–6 HrsDrive: 1–2 Hrs

We leave the mountains behind and drive back through the hills of Baitadi and Dadeldhura, then down to the plains at Dhangadhi, the largest city in far-western Nepal. It is a long day in the vehicle, with tea and lunch stops along the way, and a comfortable hotel and hot shower at the end.

Stay: HotelDrive: 9–10 Hrs

We drive east along the East-West Highway across the Terai, passing farmland, rivers and the edges of Bardiya and Banke, to Lumbini. We arrive in the evening and check in to our hotel near the sacred site.

Stay: HotelDrive: 9–10 Hrs

A slower day in Lumbini, the birthplace of the Buddha and a UNESCO World Heritage Site. We visit the Maya Devi Temple, built over the spot where the Buddha is believed to have been born, the Ashoka Pillar and the sacred pond, then explore the Monastic Zone, where countries from around the world have built monasteries in their own architectural styles. Many visitors enjoy getting around by bicycle or e-rickshaw.

Stay: HotelActivity: Sightseeing

Our final drive climbs north from Butwal along the Siddhartha Highway, through the hills around Tansen and down to the Kali Gandaki before rising again to Pokhara. The trip ends here, with the lakeside and the Annapurna range waiting after two weeks in the far west.

Drive: 6–7 Hrs
